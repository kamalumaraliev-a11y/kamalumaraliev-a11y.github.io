StudioMD growth snapshot

StudioMD is saturated on paid Search and absent from Google's two other doors: the map pack, where its organic share of the top three for med spa is 0 to 2% within 1.5 to 6 miles of eight of nine studios, and Local Services Ads, which it does not run. The plan funds seven paid channels; the first ninety days go to those two doors, because they reach the same searchers the ads already pay for, at a fraction of the cost.

  • Budget: $1.6M a year, 8% of $20M: 55% ads, 45% local, retention and content.
  • Ads: $880k across seven channels at $195 a new patient buys 4,510 new patients a year, 44% of the 10,250 that $20M takes ($20M at the $527 average visit, 27% of visits being new patients, per AmSpa). Maps, referrals and members supply the other 56%.
  • The gap: on Google Maps, StudioMD's organic share of the top three for med spa is 0 to 2% within 1.5 to 6 miles of eight of nine studios; Local Services Ads is not running; ChatGPT named it 0 times in 27 answers. 76% of people who search for something nearby visit a business within a day (Google, 2016); that is the demand going to competitors today.
  • Returning patients: 73% of med-spa visits are repeat (AmSpa) and members retain at about 95% against 60% for non-members; the membership and the Give $50/Get $50 referral program exist but are hidden. Surfacing them is the cheapest growth on this page.
  • First 30 days: four listings re-filed as Medical spa, services and reviews on every profile, LSA at SoHo and Midtown, Google consolidated from 400 campaigns to about 36. The budget

$1.6M is 8% of $20M; the industry average is about 7% and practices adding locations run 8 to 12%. 55% goes to ads, 45% to the local, retention, content, test and tools work (15, 12, 10, 5 and 3%). At $195 a new patient pays for the ad on the first visit at any price on the menu.

Google captures demand, Meta creates it; both are judged on cost per booked patient. Google consolidated to about 36 campaigns, Meta split by location, YouTube funded, Maps ads with their own budget per studio. Skipped: Yelp Ads until the 3.5-star Yelp page is fixed, Groupon (deal hunters, 50% cut), and radio, print and outdoor (no studio-level attribution).

Darien and Glen Mills get ads once they are bookable five days a week; Larchmont gets reviews before ads. The seven studios still to open take the remaining $340k, $7,000 a month in their first six months.

Four findings

Two studios cannot take a new patient for a week; where the calendar is open it is wide open (23 free slots on Larchmont's first day). Chairs, not clicks. Provider days at Darien and Glen Mills come before ad dollars, and the phone numbers need fixing: Brooklyn and Midtown list SoHo's, Darien lists Larchmont's.

Weight loss is the highest-revenue patient on the menu and sits at item 15 of the booking menu. The membership price differs between two pages, Unlimited Laser is buried on the men's page, the referral program is three menus deep, and 41% of the store shows a strikethrough; Peachy, at the same $425 Xeomin price, shows none and gets 40% of its clients from referrals. One membership price, the referral link on every receipt and text, strikethroughs off, and Unlimited Laser with its own page, ad and lifetime touch-up guarantee.

Every studio is out-reviewed by its busiest neighbour, 5 to 24 times over, and reviews set map-pack and LSA rank. A review request at every checkout, tagged to the provider.

The organic equivalent of impression share: StudioMD holds one of the map pack's three slots for med spa at 0 to 2% of spots within 1.5 miles of SoHo and Midtown, 2 miles of Brooklyn and 4 to 6 miles of the suburban studios. Only Mendham has a real share, 41%. Midtown has 137 reviews, more than the local leader's 101, and shows at one spot in 49. These are the same searchers the Search ads pay for.

Four listings are not filed as a medical spa: Garden City, White Plains and Mendham as Skin care clinic, Brooklyn as Laser hair removal service. Midtown is filed correctly; its problems are a 4.1 rating, the lowest of the nine, no services menu, and a pack full of keyword-stuffed spam listings. Weight loss appears at none of the 49 spots at seven of nine studios. Off Google: ChatGPT 0 of 27, Google AI Mode 2 of 27; Apple Maps shows SoHo and Brooklyn with a 3.5 and a 4.0 Yelp rating and no category, and no Midtown at all.

None of the fix is media spend: the category changes below, services and prices on all nine profiles, a review request at every checkout, spam listings reported, Apple Business Connect and Bing Places completed, the closed Queens listing removed, then LSA at SoHo and Midtown.

Local Services Ads, in plain terms

Local Services Ads is the box of three businesses above everything else on a search like "laser hair removal near me": name, stars, badge, call button. It is not part of Google Ads: its own account per listing, paid per lead rather than per click, ranked on reviews, response time and distance rather than bid, and Search ads run underneath it. Getting in takes the provider's license and the business registration, two to four weeks per listing, and in healthcare no ad runs before the badge; StudioMD fits Weight loss service and Hair removal service. The organic fixes take one to three months to show, while LSA ranks on reviews already earned, so SoHo (170) and Midtown (137) can sit above the pack within a month at about $130 a booked patient. Start there.

Why more budget buys the same leads

With 400 campaigns, most of Google's bidding is guessing, and guessing pays more for the same searches: more money, same leads, higher price. About 36 campaigns, one per studio per service line with audiences kept inside, then the segments that bring new searchers: weight loss, men, brides, surgical and financing, commuters vs residents, Russian, Spanish and South Asian speakers, 40+ vs 20s, lapsed patients. The check is in Google Ads: how often each campaign did not show for lack of budget versus being outbid.
